01

What the lab measures

NeuroEngineering sits between the operating theatre and the sequencer. Tissue comes out of surgery, goes into culture or onto a slide, and we read what the cells are doing with single-cell, spatial, and physiological measurements.

  1. Spatial organization of gliomas

    How IDH-mutant gliomas reorganize as they progress (Cancer Cell 2026). Hypoxic niches that drive transcriptional plasticity in low-grade glioma. Iron-metabolism stress programs that prime invasive mesenchymal glioblastoma states (Neuro-Oncology 2026).

  2. Tumor immune microenvironment

    Cell-type specific myeloid responses to hypoxia and acidosis in glioblastoma. Grade-independent immune subgroups and a T-cell recruitment deficit in meningioma (Brain and Spine 2026). Spatially resolved T-cell receptor profiling.

  3. Human brain tissue models

    Human brain slice cultures as a translational platform (Life Science Alliance 2025). Injection-free, spatially controlled tumor delivery with soft cryogels (Biomaterials Advances 2026). Tumoroids checked against the tissue they came from.

  4. Neural implants and brain microinjury

    MicroFace: automated microglial morphometry that resolves spatial zones and bifurcated recovery trajectories after microinjury (iScience 2026). Biomarkers of the tissue response to chronic cortical electrodes. What flexible probes do to the transcriptome and the physiology around them.

  5. CSF and spectroscopic diagnostics

    CSF proteomics across spontaneous intracranial hypotension and other neurological disease. FTIR spectroscopy with machine learning for CSF leak and contrast-agent detection (DiaQnos, BMBF-funded).

  6. Neurosurgical translation

    Riboflavin–UVA crosslinking to strengthen dura mater (PLOS ONE 2026). Platelet-rich fibrin for dural closure and as an epidural patch for post-dural-puncture headache. Safety of cortical access-point biopsies during shunt surgery.

02

Tools

Open-source, mostly Rust, built because the existing route was slow or loaded everything into memory. Each line is the command as you would actually type it.

karnaTranscriptomic analysis engine. Single-cell, spatial, bulk, cross-condition. Out-of-core, seeded, content-addressed provenance DAG.
karna pipeline -i data.h5ad -o results/
karna spatial-map --reference results/ --query visium_sample/ --obs-column cell_type -o spatial/

marker FC vs Seurat r = 1.000 · GBMap 338K cells in 4 min vs 25

h5utilsQuery, validate, subset, split, merge, diff h5ad files without ever loading X.
h5utils query atlas.h5ad --obs --columns cell_type --value-counts
h5utils subset atlas.h5ad --genes markers.txt -o subset.h5ad
peek-seriesTwelve CLIs that read headers or stream one pass over FASTQ, BAM, VCF, BED, GTF, 10x, h5ad, CSV, JSON. Cost stays bounded with file size.
peek sample.bam   # auto-detects the format
fqpeek reads.fq.gz --json
atmanProteomics from Olink, SomaScan, MaxQuant, DIA-NN or Spectronaut, same commands on all of them. Every command writes a SHA-256 sidecar.
atman ingest-matrix --matrix protein_groups.tsv --samples sample_metadata.tsv --output-dir out
atman de --input-dir out --output-dir out --test welch-t --groups Case-Control
mpx2nwbAlpha Omega .mpx electrophysiology to NWB 2.7, in one step instead of three Python packages.
mpx2nwb --batch ./raw --outdir ./nwb --subject-field 2 --jobs 5

3.6× compression · merges split segments · refuses to merge across gaps

MicroFace / Py_MicrofaceSegmentation and morphometry of microglia from IBA1 images: 79 parameters per cell, 400,000 cells, four phenotypes, three injury zones. The method behind the iScience paper.

Also: kaRna_R (karna as a Seurat accelerator), karnaplot, GliomaEcology, SpatialNiches, scElectro, stTrace_R, FreiSpec, FastQRescue, cloupe-to-10x. Everything else is on the lab org and my profile.
