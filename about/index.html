The lab

Four doctoral researchers, an organotypic human brain-slice platform run under an established ethical and governance framework, and experimental access to fresh human brain tissue through the Department of Neurosurgery. Live imaging, electrophysiology, spatial and single-cell transcriptomics, quantitative pathology, and the computation to hold them together. The group shares a GitHub organisation, 3DBM & NE, with the 3DBM group led by Vidhya M. Ravi.

Supervision runs on five commitments: one central aim and one principal platform fixed at the start; the biological replicate and statistical unit named before data are collected; criteria for continuing, redirecting, or stopping an experiment written down before the result exists; students expected to defend their comparator, state what their method cannot establish, and report negative results; and, for anything involving human tissue, training in consent, provenance, governance, and the line between care and research.

Teaching

  • Mapping the Tumor in Space: Spatial Transcriptomics in Glioma. Lecture at Spatial Biology in Practice, Neurozentrum Freiburg, 2026. Materials on GitHub.
  • Doctoral training in human tissue models, electrophysiology, spatial and single-cell genomics, and quantitative image analysis, 2018 to present.
  • Approach. Begin with the biological comparison, not the software. Define the question, the control, the unit of analysis, and the observation that would contradict the interpretation. Then introduce the computational method as the instrument for a question already made precise. Documented analytical failures, pseudoreplication, a wrong comparator, a model that cannot represent the variable, are the most useful teaching material there is.
