research

Glial signalling in intact human tissue

The programme is defined by its method more than by any one disease: measure function, resolve spatial molecular state, perturb the candidate mechanism, and let computation choose the next experiment.

01

The question

How do glial cells receive, integrate, and transmit signals in living human brain tissue, and how do those signalling states change what the tissue does? The central premise is that intercellular communication is spatially organised and is therefore incompletely represented after dissociation. Tumours, amyloid pathology, and chronically implanted devices are distinct perturbations, but each elicits a glial response that is communicated to neighbouring cells. The recurring test is whether that response is merely associated with injury or actively determines the subsequent state of the tissue.

If the response is causal, it is a target: in glioblastoma, a way to break the tumour's hold on the cells around it; after an implant, a way to keep an electrode working; in amyloid disease, a way to slow spread before neurons are lost.

04

Three platforms

Living human tissue

Organotypic human brain-slice culture, co-established as a framework for neuro-oncology ({{L:Human organotypic brain slice|Life Science Alliance 2019}}) and later given its translational and ethical framework ({{L:Human brain slice cultures: translational|Life Science Alliance 2025}}). It preserves architecture, permits targeted perturbation in living cortex, and allows molecular analysis of the same specimen after a functional experiment. A companion protocol recovers viable tumour cells from culture for single-cell transcriptomics ({{L:Isolation and profiling|STAR Protocols 2023}}).

Molecular and spatial measurement

Spatially resolved T-cell-receptor sequencing links immune repertoires to tissue location ({{L:High-sensitive spatially|Nature Communications 2023}}). Spatial multi-omics showed tumour and host to be bidirectionally interdependent in glioblastoma ({{L:Spatially resolved multi-omics|Cancer Cell 2022}}), and the same lens now follows the evolving organisation of IDH-mutant glioma ({{L:Spatial analysis reveals the evolving|Cancer Cell 2026}}).

Quantitative image analysis

An early result set the rule: artefact-blanking choices alter spike-train statistics, so the analytical instrument must be validated as part of the experiment ({{L:When the ostrich|Frontiers in Neuroscience 2018}}). MicroFace applies that rule to morphology, turning microglial form into a high-dimensional measurement and resolving spatial response trajectories after microinjury ({{L:MicroFace|iScience 2026}}).

05

What the work has shown

Astrocytes signal

Tumour-associated reactive astrocytes help build an immunosuppressive environment ({{L:Tumor-associated reactive|Nature Communications 2019}}), and astrocyte-derived chitinase-3-like-1 activates MAPK signalling ({{L:Astrogliosis releases|Cancers 2019}}). The developmental trajectory of human astrocytes shows where maturation diverges in glioblastoma ({{L:Mapping the developmental|Nature Cell Biology 2025}}).

Myeloid cells set immune function

Myeloid interleukin-10 mediates T-cell dysfunction in glioblastoma ({{L:T-cell dysfunction|Nature Communications 2022}}). ZBTB18 regulates cytokine expression and microglia/macrophage recruitment ({{L:ZBTB18|Communications Biology 2024}}), and SMAC mimetics reshape the microglial phenotype ({{L:SMAC mimetic|Cell Death & Disease 2024}}). Outside oncology, microglia carry amyloid-β into unaffected tissue ({{L:Microglia contribute|Nature Neuroscience 2022}}).

Connectivity decides therapy

Gap-junction inhibition sensitises primary glioblastoma cells to temozolomide ({{L:Inhibition of gap junctions|Cancers 2019}}), meclofenamate decouples functional tumour networks ({{L:Meclofenamate|Neuro-Oncology 2021}}), sialic-acid metabolism governs transcellular connectivity ({{L:Sialic acid|Neuro-Oncology 2023}}), and blocking metabotropic glutamate receptor III sensitises glioblastoma to alkylating chemotherapy ({{L:Inhibition of metabotropic|Cell Death & Disease 2021}}).

The implant work closes the loop: a chronically implanted flexible probe elicits a related glial programme, measured transcriptome-wide ({{L:Transcriptional characterization of the glial|Biomaterials 2021}}), with the physical interface resolved by synchrotron tomography ({{L:Quantitative synchrotron|Scientific Reports 2019}}) and electrochemical sensing ({{L:New life for old wires|Journal of Neural Engineering 2020}}). An engineered material and a tumour provoke the same kind of answer from the same cells.

06

Where it goes next: functional spatial signalling genomics

A defined perturbation, a physiological consequence, and a molecular-spatial state, all measured in the same human specimen. Three linked aims.

  1. Define functional glial signalling states in intact human cortex. Pair patch-clamp and multi-electrode recordings with spatial molecular profiling. The statistical unit is the donor; cells and locations are nested measurements. Early observations in tumour-infiltrated cortex suggest function is not predicted by the abundance of single channel transcripts, so the work tests receptor and channel complexes, accessory subunits, and cell–cell interactions rather than equating expression with physiology.
  2. Establish causality by targeted perturbation. Perturb candidate pathways in organotypic human slices and judge them by concordant effects on cell state, tissue function, viability, and spatial organisation. Start with axes where perturbation has already changed therapeutic response: metabotropic glutamate receptor signalling and intercellular coupling.
  3. Use machine learning to choose the experiment, not to classify the sample. Human tissue permits few interventions per donor. Models rank experiments by expected information gain from prior molecular data, uncertainty, feasibility, and explicit falsifiers, and their recommendations are recorded before the result is known, so the computation is auditable.
