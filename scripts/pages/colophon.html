How this site is built

Static HTML, one stylesheet, and a script of twenty lines that remembers whether you chose dark or light. Nothing is fetched from a third party: no fonts, no analytics, no cookies, no embeds. The pages are generated by a Python script from page bodies and a publications file, and the build is deterministic: the same inputs produce the same bytes.

The papers list began as a Google Scholar export. Conference abstracts, posters, and the thesis were set aside; every remaining entry had its DOI resolved through Crossref, preferring the journal version over preprints. Affiliations, countries, and citation counts come from OpenAlex, keyed by DOI. The trajectory strip classifies every Scholar entry by topic with a set of title rules kept in the build script. The world map is an Equal Earth projection of outlines from the R maps package, simplified until the file was small enough to inline.

The site keeps the rules the laboratory keeps. A claim is shown at the level of the evidence behind it: linked when published, plain when it is still a question. Figures state where their data came from and when. Nothing is shown that cannot be traced to a file in the repository, and the files are published at /data/.
