The lab

NeuroEngineering works in living human tissue because the thing it studies, signalling between glial, immune, neuronal, and malignant cells, is spatially organised and does not survive dissociation intact. The lab holds an organotypic human brain-slice platform under an established ethical and governance framework, with tissue arriving directly from the Department of Neurosurgery, and reads it with live imaging, electrophysiology, spatial and single-cell transcriptomics, quantitative pathology, and the computation that holds those measurements together. It shares a GitHub organisation, 3DBM & NE, with the 3DBM group led by Vidhya M. Ravi.

What it stands for: the instrument is validated as part of the experiment; the biological replicate and the statistical unit are named before data are collected; the criteria for continuing, redirecting, or stopping an experiment are written down before the result exists; a method's limits are stated alongside its findings, and negative results are reported; and anything involving human tissue carries training in consent, provenance, governance, and the line between care and research.

Teaching

  • Mapping the Tumor in Space: Spatial Transcriptomics in Glioma. Lecture at Spatial Biology in Practice, Neurozentrum Freiburg, 2026. Materials on GitHub.
  • Doctoral training in human tissue models, electrophysiology, spatial and single-cell genomics, and quantitative image analysis, 2018 to present.
  • Approach. Begin with the biological comparison, not the software. Define the question, the control, the unit of analysis, and the observation that would contradict the interpretation. Then introduce the computational method as the instrument for a question already made precise. Documented analytical failures, pseudoreplication, a wrong comparator, a model that cannot represent the variable, are the most useful teaching material there is.
